# Credit Risk Survival Modeling with Physics-Informed Neural Networks

## Overview

This project frames **credit default as a time-to-event problem** rather than a binary classification task, using ~2.25 million real-world Lending Club loans. Three modeling paradigms are compared end-to-end:

| Approach | Method |
|----------|--------|
| Classical ML Baselines | Logistic Regression, XGBoost |
| Data-Only Survival NN | PyTorch neural network with survival likelihood |
| Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) | Hazard network + survival ODE constraint |

The core argument: **predictive accuracy alone is insufficient** for credit models used in regulated environments (Basel III, IFRS 9). Models must produce time-consistent, monotonically valid probability-of-default (PD) term structures. This project demonstrates why, and how a PINN enforces that consistency.

---

## Results

**Discrimination (AUC) on held-out test set (30% of 2.25M loans):**

| Model | AUC @ 12m | AUC @ 24m | AUC @ 36m |
|-------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Logistic Regression | 0.692 | 0.680 | 0.675 |
| XGBoost | **0.707** | **0.704** | **0.705** |

Classical models achieve comparable AUC across horizons, but violate a critical property: **P(default by 12m) is not guaranteed ≤ P(default by 24m)**. This inconsistency is demonstrated visually in Notebook 02.

**Survival curve consistency:**
- The data-only NN produces non-monotonic survival curves — `S(t)` can increase over time, violating survival theory
- The PINN enforces the ODE constraint, guaranteeing smooth, monotonically decreasing survival curves valid for extrapolation

---

## Motivation

Standard credit models predict default within a single fixed horizon. This has three fundamental problems:

1. **Censoring bias** — loans still active at observation cutoff are ignored, not treated as right-censored
2. **Horizon inconsistency** — independently trained models at 12m/24m/36m can produce logically impossible PDs (e.g., P(default by 24m) < P(default by 12m))
3. **No monotonicity guarantee** — nothing prevents a data-only model from producing survival curves that increase over time

Survival analysis addresses (1) and (2) natively. The PINN addresses (3) by embedding the survival ODE as a soft constraint:

$$\frac{dS(t)}{dt} = -\lambda(t) \cdot S(t)$$

where $\lambda(t) \geq 0$ is the hazard rate and $S(t)$ is the survival probability.

## Methodology

### 1. Survival Data Preparation (Notebook 01)

- Observation time: `T = last_payment_date − issue_date` (months)
- Event indicator: `δ = 1` for "Charged Off", "Default", "Late (31–120 days)"; `δ = 0` otherwise (right-censored)
- Features used: `annual_inc`, `dti`, `loan_amnt`, `int_rate`, `grade`, `home_ownership`, `emp_length`, `term`

### 2. Classical ML Baselines (Notebook 02)

- Binary horizon labels: defaulted within H months, for H ∈ {12, 24, 36}
- Logistic Regression (with StandardScaler) and XGBoost trained independently per horizon
- Demonstrates PD inconsistency across horizons — motivates the survival modeling approach

### 3. Data-Only Survival NN (Notebook 03)

- Network maps `(x, t) → S ∈ (0, 1)`, predicting survival probability directly
- Trained with a simplified binary likelihood (no censoring adjustment)
- Produces non-monotonic survival curves, demonstrating the need for structural constraints

### 4. Physics-Informed Neural Network (Notebook 04)

- Network models **cumulative hazard** $\Lambda(x, t) \geq 0$ directly
- Survival: $S(x, t) = e^{-\Lambda(x, t)}$
- **Physics constraint:** $\frac{d\Lambda}{dt} \geq 0$ — hazard rate is non-negative, guaranteeing $S(t)$ is monotonically decreasing
- Total loss:

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{data}} + \alpha \cdot \mathcal{L}_{\text{physics}}$$

where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}$ is the negative log-likelihood and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{physics}}$ penalises any decrease in $\Lambda$ over time at collocation points.

## Industry Context

In regulated credit risk (Basel III, IFRS 9 impairment, stress testing), models must:
- Output **PD term structures**, not just point-in-time estimates
- Satisfy **stochastic ordering**: longer horizon → higher cumulative default probability
- Be **stable under stress** and economic extrapolation
- Be **auditable**: outputs should follow known financial theory

Physics-informed constraints embed survival theory directly into the neural network, producing models that are both flexible and theoretically consistent — a principled alternative to post-hoc calibration.
